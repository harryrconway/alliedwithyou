Occupational therapy

What does an occupational therapist do?

Occupational therapists help people do the everyday things they need and want to do, such as looking after themselves, getting around, running a household, working and taking part in their community. When illness, injury, disability or ageing makes those things harder, we help find a way through.

Do I need a referral?

No. You can contact us directly if you're paying privately or using NDIS or Support at Home funding. To claim a Medicare rebate, you'll need a referral from your GP as part of a GP chronic condition management plan.

How is occupational therapy different from physiotherapy?

There is some overlap. Broadly, physiotherapists focus on movement, strength and physical function, while occupational therapists focus on the tasks and activities of daily life and how to make them possible. Many people see both.

How it works

What happens, from getting in touch to finishing up?

Everyone's path looks a little different. Some people need a single home visit. Others work with us for months. Most people go through these steps.

  1. Getting in touch. You can contact us yourself, or a family member, GP, support coordinator or other professional can refer you. Email us or get in touch through the website, whatever's easiest.
  2. A conversation first. We'll get in touch to talk about what's happening, what you'd like help with and how sessions will be funded. If we're not the right fit, we'll say so and suggest other options.
  3. Paperwork, kept simple. Before the first appointment we send a short service agreement and consent form. They explain what we'll do, what it costs and how your information is handled. See your first appointment for what to have ready.
  4. Assessment. At the first appointment we get to know you and how things are going day to day. We look at what's working, what isn't, and what you'd like to be different.
  5. Setting goals. Together we agree on goals, written in your words, and a plan for working towards them.
  6. Therapy and recommendations. Depending on your goals, this might be regular sessions, equipment trials, changes around the home, or advice for the people who support you.
  7. Reports, when they're needed. If a funding body, doctor or other professional needs information from us, we write it up clearly and check it with you before it's sent.
  8. Reviewing and finishing up. We check progress regularly. When you've reached your goals, we plan to finish together and leave you with what you need to keep going. You're welcome back any time.
Is there a wait to start?

If there's a wait to start, we'll tell you when you first get in touch. We'd rather be honest about timing than book you in and push appointments back.

If your situation is urgent, let us know and we'll do our best to help or point you to someone who can.

Appointments

Where do appointments happen?

Usually in your home or out in the community, wherever the task you are working on actually happens. Some appointments can be done by telehealth.

How long is an appointment?

Therapy sessions are usually around an hour. Assessments take longer and are sometimes spread over two visits. We will let you know what to expect before each appointment.

Can a family member or carer be there?

Yes, and it often helps. Let us know who you'd like involved and we'll make sure we have your consent to talk with them.

What areas do you cover?

We visit people across the Northern Rivers in New South Wales and the Gold Coast in Queensland. Travel charges may apply further out.

Anywhere else in Australia, we can work with you by telehealth. It suits reviews, many therapy sessions and conversations with family and carers. Some things, like home safety assessments and home modification prescriptions, need us there in person.

Assessments and reports

How long does a report take?

It depends on the type of report. We'll give you an expected timeframe when we book the assessment. If you have a deadline, such as an NDIS plan reassessment, tell us early.

Who gets a copy of my report?

You do, and anyone else you've agreed to, such as your plan manager, support coordinator or GP. We won't send it anywhere without your consent unless the law requires it.

NDIS

Do I need a service agreement?

Yes. It sets out what we will provide, the rates, how invoicing works and our cancellation terms, so everyone knows where they stand.

Can you help before my plan reassessment?

Yes. We can write a progress report or functional capacity report to support your reassessment. Let us know the date as soon as you have it.

Fees and funding

How much does it cost?

Fees depend on the service and how it is funded. Our fees page lists current rates, and we always confirm costs before we start.

Can I claim from my health fund?

Many extras policies include occupational therapy. Check with your fund about your cover and rebate. We provide an itemised receipt you can claim with.

Can I use a Medicare GP plan?

Yes. With a referral from your GP under a chronic condition management plan, Medicare pays a rebate towards up to five allied health sessions a year in total. You pay the difference between our fee and the rebate.

Can I use my Support at Home package?

Yes. We work with your package provider, who arranges payment for the services you have agreed to.

Cancellations

What if I need to cancel?

Please let us know as soon as you can so we can offer the time to someone else. Late cancellations may be charged. See fees and cancellations for the details.

What if you need to cancel?

Occasionally we may need to reschedule because of illness or an emergency. We'll contact you as early as possible and find another time that suits.
