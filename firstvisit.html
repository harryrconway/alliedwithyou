How it works/Your first appointment

Your first appointment

What happens at a first appointment, who can be there, and what to have ready, so there are no surprises.

Before we meet

Before the first appointment we send a short service agreement and consent form. They explain what we'll do, what it costs and how your information is handled. Questions are always welcome.

On the day

At the first appointment we get to know you and how things are going day to day. We look at what's working, what isn't, and what you'd like to be different.

At home, we may ask to see how you manage the everyday things you'd like help with, in the places you actually do them, like the bathroom, the kitchen or the front steps.

Assessments take longer than therapy sessions and are sometimes spread over two visits. A private initial assessment is up to two hours, including a short summary of recommendations. We'll let you know what to expect before the appointment.

Who can be there

A family member, carer or support worker is welcome, and it often helps. Let us know who you'd like involved and we'll make sure we have your consent to talk with them.

If your appointment is by telehealth

  • Use a phone, tablet or computer with a camera and microphone, and a steady internet connection
  • Find somewhere quiet, with good light on your face
  • Have the space you'd like to talk about nearby, if you can, so you can show us
  • Keep your phone handy, in case the video drops out

If you need to cancel

Please let us know as early as you can, so we can offer the time to someone else. For private clients, cancellations with less than 24 hours notice may be charged. For NDIS participants, short notice cancellations are charged as set out in your service agreement. See fees and cancellations.
