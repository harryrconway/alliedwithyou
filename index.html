Therapy that starts with what you want to do

Occupational therapy is about the everyday things. Getting showered and dressed, cooking a meal, moving safely around your home, getting back to work or out into your community.

When something makes those things harder, an occupational therapist works with you to find a way through. We take time to understand how you live, what's getting in the way and what you'd like to change.

Then we work on it together, with practical advice, equipment, changes around the home or therapy that builds your skills and confidence.

How we can help

Everyone's situation is different, so think of this as a starting point. If you're not sure what you need, that's fine. We can work it out with you.

  • Functional capacity assessments

    A detailed look at how you manage daily tasks, with clear recommendations about the support you need.

  • Daily living and independence

    Building skills and routines for self care, cooking, getting around and managing your day.

  • Home safety and access

    A visit to look at falls risks, access and layout, with practical changes that suit your home.

  • Assistive technology and equipment

    Advice on equipment that makes things easier, from shower chairs to kitchen aids, and help getting it set up.

  • Goal focused therapy

    Regular sessions working towards goals you set, with progress reviewed along the way.

  • Reports and recommendations

    Clear, well supported reports for funding bodies, plan reassessments and other professionals.

Who we see

We work with people living with disability, injury, illness or the changes that come with getting older. Family members, carers and support coordinators are welcome to get in touch on someone's behalf.

  • Older people living at home
  • People living with disability
  • People recovering from illness or injury
  • People with chronic health conditions
  • Families and carers
  • Support coordinators

What happens when you get in touch

  1. You get in touch

    Send us an email or an enquiry through the website. You can contact us yourself, or a family member, GP or support coordinator can refer you.

  2. We have a chat

    We'll get in touch to talk about what's going on, what you're hoping for and how your sessions will be funded. If we're not the right fit, we'll help you find someone who is.

  3. Your first appointment

    We meet you at home, out in the community or by telehealth, and start working out the best way forward together.

Paying for therapy

Most of our clients pay privately, sometimes with help from a health fund, a Medicare GP plan or a Support at Home package. We also work with NDIS participants whose plans are self managed or plan managed.

Before you start, we'll explain the fees and give you a written agreement, so there are no surprises.
