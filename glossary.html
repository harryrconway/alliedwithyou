Words we use

Plain English explanations of the words that come up in occupational therapy, the NDIS, aged care and Medicare. Across the website, words with a dotted line under them open a short explanation too.

Occupational therapy
Help to do the everyday things you need and want to do, like looking after yourself, getting around, running a household, working and taking part in your community.
Assistive technology
Equipment and devices that make everyday tasks easier or safer, such as a shower chair, walking frame or wheelchair. Often shortened to AT.
Home modifications
Changes to a home that make it safer or easier to use, like grab rails, ramps, a step free shower or wider doorways.
Functional capacity assessment
A thorough look at how you manage personal care, household tasks, getting around and taking part in your community, written up with clear recommendations. Often used to support NDIS funding.
Telehealth
An appointment by video call, so we don't need to be in the same place.
Service agreement
A short document that sets out what we'll provide, the rates, how invoicing works and our cancellation terms.
Consent
Your permission. We ask for it before collecting or sharing your information, for example with your GP or plan manager.
Referral
A request from someone, like your GP or support coordinator, for us to see you. You don't need one to see us privately.
NDIS
The National Disability Insurance Scheme, which funds supports for eligible people with a permanent and significant disability.
NDIS plan
The document that sets out a participant's goals and the funding they have for supports.
Capacity Building
The part of an NDIS plan that funds supports to build skills and independence. Occupational therapy usually comes from Improved Daily Living within it.
Improved Daily Living
The Capacity Building category that usually covers occupational therapy assessments, therapy sessions, report writing and related travel.
Capital budget
The part of an NDIS plan that funds assistive technology and home modifications.
Plan managed
A plan manager pays providers from your NDIS plan for you. We send our invoices to them.
Self managed
You manage your own NDIS funds, pay providers and claim through the my NDIS portal or app.
Support coordinator
Someone funded through an NDIS plan to help a participant understand and use their plan, and connect with providers.
Plan reassessment
When the NDIS reviews a plan and decides on new funding. Reports from therapists can help show what support is needed.
My Aged Care
The Australian Government's starting point for aged care, where you apply for an assessment and look for providers.
Aged care assessment
A free assessment, usually in your home, to work out what aged care help you're eligible for.
Support at Home
The government program that funds most aged care help at home. It began on 1 November 2025.
Assistive Technology and Home Modifications scheme
Funds equipment and home modifications for people on Support at Home, separately from their quarterly budget. Home modifications must be prescribed by an occupational therapist.
Restorative Care Pathway
A short burst of intensive allied health or nursing through Support at Home, usually up to 16 weeks, to help you regain function after a change in your health.
Chronic condition management plan
A plan from your GP that lets you claim a Medicare rebate towards up to five allied health sessions a year, shared across all the allied health professionals you see.
Extras cover
Private health insurance that can pay part of the cost of services like occupational therapy, depending on your policy.
Ahpra
The Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency. It keeps the public register of registered health practitioners, including occupational therapists.
