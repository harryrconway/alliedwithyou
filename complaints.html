Complaints and feedback

We want to hear what's working and what isn't. Feedback helps us do better, and raising a concern will never affect the service you receive.

How to give feedback

You can tell your therapist directly, call or email us, or use the contact form. A family member, carer, advocate or support coordinator can also raise something on your behalf.

What happens when you make a complaint

  • We'll acknowledge your complaint within two business days
  • We'll talk with you to understand what happened and what you'd like to see happen
  • We'll look into it fairly and keep you updated
  • We'll tell you what we found and what we'll do about it, usually within 21 days

Your complaint will be handled confidentially, and we keep a record of it so we can learn from it.

If you're not satisfied

If we can't resolve your concern, you can contact an independent body:

  • Health Care Complaints Commission, for services in New South Wales
  • Office of the Health Ombudsman, for services in Queensland
  • NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission, for NDIS participants
  • Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency, about a registered practitioner's conduct

Contact details for each body, checked before launch

Getting support

If you'd like help making a complaint, you can bring a support person or advocate. Disability advocacy services can help NDIS participants at no cost.

Raise something with us

Call, email or use the contact form. Tell us as much or as little as you're comfortable with to begin with.
